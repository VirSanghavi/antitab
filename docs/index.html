antitab v1.2.0 · free · open source

Make a site think you never left the tab.

Switch away and a page is told about it: the video pauses, timers slow to a crawl, idle countdowns start, sessions expire. Antitab quietly tells the page you are still right there. Nothing to install. It is just a bookmark.

Drag this to your bookmarks bar

Antitab

Do not click it here. Drag it up to the strip of bookmarks under the address bar. Cannot see that strip? Press Ctrl Shift B.

  1. Drag the black Antitab button to your bookmarks bar.

    You only ever do this once.

  2. Open the page you want left alone.

    A lecture, a stream, a music player, a dashboard that logs you out.

  3. Click the Antitab bookmark once.

    A small black bar confirms it. Now switch tabs all you like. Click it again to turn it off.

I cannot drag things onto my bookmarks bar

Copy the code instead, then make the bookmark by hand.

Then

  1. Press Ctrl Shift O to open the bookmark manager.
  2. Click the three dots in the top right, then Add new bookmark.
  3. Name it Antitab, paste the copied code into the URL box, and save.
  4. Drag it onto your bookmarks bar so it is easy to reach.
The video still pauses

Two things usually explain it:

  • The thing sits inside another site. A bookmark only reaches the page you are on, not a video embedded from somewhere else. Open the video on its own site and click Antitab there.
  • You clicked Antitab before the page finished loading. Press play first, then click the bookmark.

Antitab also cannot help where the pausing happens on the company's servers rather than in the page. Some exam and training platforms work that way on purpose.

Clicking the bookmark does nothing at all

A few pages block bookmarks like this one: bank sites, the Chrome Web Store and Chrome's own settings pages. There is nothing to be done there. On an ordinary site you should always see a small black confirmation bar at the bottom.

On a school or work Chromebook?

This bookmark is the version for you. Managed Chromebooks usually block installing extensions and turn off developer tools, and a bookmark needs neither, so it keeps working where the extension cannot be installed.

If bookmarks are locked down too, your administrator has blocked this deliberately, and there is no way around that. Ask them.

Prefer the proper extension?

On a computer where you can install extensions, the extension version is better: it works automatically on every visit, with no clicking, and it survives page reloads. It is in the same repository.

What it actually does

Web pages can ask the browser “is my tab in front right now?”, and Chrome volunteers plenty more besides. Antitab answers “yes, always”: it hides the moment you switch away, keeps the page's timers and animation running at full speed instead of being throttled, synthesises a little pointer movement so idle countdowns never start, and presses play again if a video pauses regardless.

It sends nothing anywhere. No accounts, no tracking, no network requests, no data collected. It is a few hundred lines of JavaScript that run only on the page you clicked it on, and you can read every one of them.
